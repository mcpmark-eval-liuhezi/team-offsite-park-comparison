## 1. Acadia National Park (Maine)

**Description:** Acadia National Park protects the natural beauty of the highest rocky headlands along the Atlantic coastline of the United States, an abundance of habitats, and a rich cultural heritage. At 4 million visits a year, it's one of the top 10 most-visited national parks in the United States. Visitors enjoy 27 miles of historic motor roads, 158 miles of hiking trails, and 45 miles of carriage roads. Located on Mount Desert Island, about 5 miles south of Bar Harbor. Park is open year-round; entrance fee $35/vehicle (7 days), $30 motorcycle, $20/person on foot or bike.

**Main activities (per NPS):** Hiking (front-country), biking on the 45-mile carriage roads, stargazing/astronomy, boating & boat tours, fresh/saltwater fishing (fly, freshwater, saltwater), picnicking, boat/bus shuttle guided tours, horseback riding, geocaching, wildlife/bird watching, winter options (cross-country skiing, snowshoeing, snowmobiling, ice skating) — though first snowfall typically begins in November.

**Current alerts/closures (as of NPS data pull):**
- ⚠️ **Cadillac Summit Road CLOSED to all users Nov 9–25** (construction resuming for an accessible pedestrian path between East and West parking lots) — directly overlaps our window. Hiking trails on Cadillac Mountain remain open, but you cannot drive up Cadillac.
- Park Loop Road closes to motor vehicles for the season Dec 1 (except Jordan Pond via Jordan Pond Road and Sand Beach via Schooner Head Road).
- Eastern section of Beech Mountain Loop Trail closed for repairs (alternates available: outer loop, Beech South Ridge Trail, Beech West Ridge Trail).
- Note: first snowfall typically begins in November; winter temps 14–35°F.

**Campground options (we want to camp at least one night):**
- **Blackwoods Campground** (281 sites, $30/night, reservation-only via recreation.gov) — wooded sites within a 10-minute walk of the ocean; flush toilets, running water, dump station. ⚠️ **Open seasonally, usually early May to mid-October** — mid-November camping is questionable.
- **Seawall Campground** (202 sites, $22–30/night, reservation-only) — west side of Mount Desert Island, short walk to coastline. ⚠️ **Open seasonally, typically Wednesday before Memorial Day to Columbus Day** — closed for the season by our dates.
- **Schoodic Woods Campground** (89 sites, $22–40/night, reservation-only) — on the Schoodic Peninsula, ~60–70 min from Bar Harbor; 78 sites with electrical hookups. ⚠️ **Open seasonally from the Wednesday before Memorial Day Weekend until Columbus Day** — also closed for our dates.
- **Duck Harbor Campground** (5 lean-to sites, $20/night, mailboat access only) — remote, not vehicle-accessible.
- **Bottom line:** All main Acadia campgrounds are seasonal and typically close by mid-October. Mid-November camping at Acadia is unreliable.

**Events Nov 9–15:** **None listed by NPS** for our window.

## 2. Grand Canyon National Park (Arizona) — South Rim

**Description:** Entirely within the state of Arizona, the park encompasses 278 miles (447 km) of the Colorado River and adjacent uplands. Located on the ancestral homelands of 11 present-day Tribal Communities, Grand Canyon is one of the most spectacular examples of erosion anywhere in the world — a mile-deep canyon unmatched in the incomparable vistas it offers visitors from both north and south rims. South Rim is open all year, located 60 miles north of Williams, AZ. Entrance fee: $35/vehicle (7 days), $30 motorcycle, $20/person on foot or bike.

**Main activities (per NPS):** Hiking (front-country and backcountry), scenic driving (Auto/Driving), stargazing/astronomy, boat tours, car/ATV, guided tours, horseback trekking, whitewater rafting, fishing, wildlife/bird watching, museum exhibits, Junior Ranger Program, campground activities, camping (front-country, backcountry, horse camp). Elevations range from ~2,500 ft at the bottom to 8,400 ft on the North Rim — weather varies significantly; winters are cold and snowy, conditions can change rapidly.

**Current alerts/closures (as of NPS data pull):**
- ⚠️ **INNER CANYON TRAIL CLOSURES:** The entire North Kaibab Trail (including Cottonwood Campground), Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground, Silver Bridge, and Black Bridge are currently closed. Do not attempt to access closed areas.
- 🚨 **Stage 4 water restrictions & overnight lodging suspended parkwide:** Due to extensive flash flood damage to the Transcanyon Waterline, overnight accommodations are suspended and only dry camping is allowed. The park remains open for day use; food/beverage services, the Grand Canyon Clinic, and Post Office remain open. **Visitors are asked to bring water with them.** No wood/charcoal fires allowed on the South Rim and inner canyon areas; spigot access and bathroom faucets are turned off.
- ⚠️ Inner canyon water shutoffs: no potable water available in the inner canyon (Bright Angel Trail resthouses, Havasupai Gardens) since Sep 6 — carry all water or filtration.
- South Rim entrance station near Tusayan can have lines; East Entrance (Desert View) usually has shorter wait times.

**Campground options (we want to camp at least one night):**
- **Mather Campground – South Rim** (327 sites, 300 reservable via recreation.gov + **15 first-come, first-served sites that go on sale at 8 am daily** as a backstop) — **$30 family site (6 people, 3 tents, 2 vehicles), $50 group site for 7–50 people (ideal for us)**, $6 communal hiker/biker sites. Flush toilets (year-round), potable water (year-round), no hookups, no fires allowed, free dump station, laundry, Camp Store, firewood for sale. Most RV spaces are pull-through; max vehicle length 30 ft. Check in at noon, out at 11 am. This is the **only viable in-park camping option for our dates** — book via recreation.gov ASAP; 15 FCFS sites drop at 8 am daily.
- **Desert View Campground** (49 sites, $30/night) — ⚠️ **closed for the season since Oct 18, 2026**. Not available.
- **Trailer Village RV Park** (123 sites, $80–85/night) — ⚠️ **CLOSED as of August 31, 2026** due to Stage 4 water restrictions. Not available.
- **North Rim:** select areas open only Oct 1–Nov 14, so it's theoretically in-season but remote (30 miles south of Jacob Lake) and not practical for a team offsite.

**Events Nov 9–15 (per NPS calendar):**
- 🎨 **Grand Canyon Celebration of Art 2026** at historic Kolb Studio (South Rim Village) — daily through Dec 31; Kolb Studio open 8 am–6 pm. en plein air paintings by regional artists; artwork for sale online and at Kolb Studio.
- 🥾 **Free Guided Rim Walk** — 10 am daily at Bright Angel Trailhead; 60-minute paved rim walk, topics vary (animals/plants, Indigenous plant use, etc.). Perfect for Junior Rangers.
- 🪨 **Free Geology Talk** — 2:00–2:30 pm daily at Yavapai Geology Museum; 30-minute accessible ranger program on how the canyon formed (1-mile deep, up to 18 miles wide, 20+ rock layers).
- 🌌 **Evening astronomy programs** — including **"One Sky, Many Eyes" on Sat, Nov 14** with GCC Astronomer in Residence Dr. Amy Sayle (indoor presentation + outdoor sky tour, weather permitting) — a perfect campground-night finale. Other evening programs: "Celebrate the Night" and "The Sky is a Time Machine," also with Dr. Amy Sayle.

## Top Recommendation: Grand Canyon National Park (South Rim)

**Why:** Camping **is** available in November at Grand Canyon (Mather Campground, 327 sites, $50 group site for 7–50 people), whereas Acadia's campgrounds (Blackwoods, Seawall, Schoodic Woods) are typically shut for the season by mid-October — mid-November camping at Acadia is questionable. Grand Canyon offers free ranger programs (Guided Rim Walk daily at 10 am, Geology Talk daily at 2 pm), the Celebration of Art exhibit at Kolb Studio (daily through Dec 31), and evening astronomy programs (Nov 14 with Dr. Amy Sayle) that line up exactly with our Nov 9–15 dates and a campground night. It's about a 4.5–5 hour flight from Beijing/Guiyang to Phoenix/Flagstaff, vs. much longer for Acadia.

**Constraints to plan around:**
- No inner-canyon hiking (North Kaibab, Bright Angel, Phantom Ranch all closed) — rim trails only.
- No campfires/wood or charcoal fires; spigots and bathroom faucets turned off due to Stage 4 water restrictions — **bring your own water**.
- Overnight lodging suspended parkwide; only dry camping allowed.
- Pack for cold nights (rim elevations ~7,000 ft; pack cooler layers).
- Book Mather Campground via recreation.gov ASAP; 15 first-come-first-served sites drop at 8 am daily as a backup.
